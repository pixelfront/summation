Action Summation Sheet: Privacy Notice

Please read this before you use the app for organizing work.

The short version

What you type stays on your phone. The app has no accounts, no servers, no tracking and no analytics, and it never sends your answers anywhere. There are still a few ways your information could get out, and they're listed below. Read them before you write anything sensitive.

What the app does and doesn't do

  • Your answers are stored only in your browser on this device. They aren't uploaded, synced or backed up by the app.
  • The app makes no outside requests. The page, icons and offline copy all come from the app's own site. There are no fonts, scripts, ads or trackers from anywhere else.
  • The app works offline. Once it's installed, you can use it in airplane mode.
  • The background music is made on your phone. It's generated by the app itself, with no audio files or downloads.
  • The code is public. Anyone can read it and check these claims.

Where your information could still get out

1. Voice dictation and driving mode. These use your phone's built-in speech recognition. On iPhone, Apple may process that audio on its servers to turn it into text, under Apple's own privacy policy. Driving mode keeps the mic on for longer stretches, so more of what's said nearby may be sent this way. The app doesn't control this and can't see what Apple does with it.
If that's a concern: type sensitive answers instead of speaking them, or turn off dictation in your phone's settings.

2. Loading the app. The app is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you first open or update it, GitHub's servers see your IP address and the fact that you visited, the same as with any website. They don't see your answers.
If that's a concern: open or update it on a network you trust, or through a VPN or Tor.

3. Anyone who has your phone. Saved sheets can be read by anyone who can unlock your phone, and that includes police if the phone is seized. The app has no password of its own.
If that's a concern: use a strong passcode instead of only Face ID or Touch ID, and delete sheets once they've been reported.

4. Things you send out yourself. Copy report, export and print put your answers on the clipboard, in a file, on a printer or in a PDF. From there they go wherever you send them: a chat app, email or cloud storage. They are then protected only as well as that place protects them.
If that's a concern: share reports only through end-to-end encrypted apps, and delete exported files you no longer need.

5. Phone backups and syncing. Your phone's own backups (iCloud or a computer) may include website data such as saved sheets.
If that's a concern: check your backup settings, or clear the app's data when you're done.

Good habits for sensitive actions

  • Write as if it could be read in court. Don't record anything you wouldn't want someone else to see.
  • Use roles, initials or code names, not people's full names, phone numbers or addresses.
  • Leave out other people's personal details unless the summation really needs them.
  • Report, then delete. Once a summation has gone to your group, delete it from the app.
  • Clear everything when you need to. In Safari settings, go to Advanced, then Website Data, and remove this site. Deleting the app from your home screen also removes it.

Nothing is risk-free

This app reduces risk but doesn't remove it. Your phone, your network, your speech provider and the people you share with all affect your security. Weigh the risks for each action before you use the app.
